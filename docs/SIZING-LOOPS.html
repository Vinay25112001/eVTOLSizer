Sizing Loop Anatomy

Every stage the engine runs, in the order it runs them, for each of the six configurations — drawn from src/engine.js and src/engine/configuration.js as they are, with the source behind each formula. Nothing here is a description of what the tool should do; it is what it does.

One loop, six configurations. There is a single sizing iteration. It is NDARC's own architecture — NASA/TP-20220000355 §5-1.2 describes one sizing task for every rotorcraft type, parameterised by configuration — and a separate loop per layout would be the mistake, not the feature. What differs by configuration is which branches fire inside it, and that is what the diagrams below make visible.

winged branch — lift+cruise, tiltrotor, hybrid, hybrid-pusherrotor-borne branch — multicopter, side-by-side

1 · The whole run

Four phases. The first two run once; R2 iterates until gross weight closes; the last two evaluate the converged aircraft.

The tolerance is scaled to gross weight — 0.01·W·ε — per NDARC §5-1.2 verbatim: "0.01Wε for gross weight, maximum takeoff weight, fuel weight, jet thrust, and design rotor thrust." Until 2026-09-08 it was an absolute 10⁻⁶ kg, unreachable on the heavier layouts, which were reported as non-converged while within micrograms of the answer.

2 · Inside R2 — the sixteen stages, in order

Each iteration takes the current MTOW, rebuilds the aircraft from it, and returns the mass that aircraft would weigh. The fork at stage 1 is the configuration fork: everything downstream of it that concerns a wing is skipped on the rotor-borne layouts, and their cruise is charged at a rotor-borne L/D and efficiency instead.

Stage 4 fires only on the side-by-side. Stage 9's fraction branch applies one empty-weight fraction to every layout; NASA's own all-electric vehicles span 0.49–0.585 ex-battery, so that branch is 17% light on lift+cruise — recorded, not yet fixed. The buildup branch is the default.

4 · The formulas the loop turns on, with their sources

Only the ones that carry a citation the reader can open. The full census is in §6.

Hover power — momentum theory

P_hover = (W / η_hov) · √( DL / (2ρ) ) DL = W·(T/W) / (N · π R²) rotor diameter is a CONSEQUENCE of the disk-loading target, solved per iteration

Disk loading targets: NASA/TM-20210017971 Table 12 per vehicle. Hover download: Felker; tiltrotor download on the wing is not defaulted to zero.

Convergence — NDARC §5-1.2

converged when |m_new − MTOW| < 0.01 · W · ε ε = 10^convTolExp, default 10⁻⁶ cap 600 iterations; runaway stopped by the MTOW ceiling, not the cap

NASA/TP-20220000355 §5-1.1–5-1.2. Relaxation on W_MTO was implemented per §5-2.1, measured, and found not to help here — the fraction-model divergences are a missing root, not a repelling one.

Fuselage wetted area — Raymer, with a floor where Raymer fails

λ > 2.5 : S_wet = π·D·L·(1 − 2/λ)^(2/3)·(1 + 1/λ²) Raymer Eq 12.31 λ ≤ 2.5 : prolate spheroid, exact (1 − 2/λ) < 0 below λ = 2 → NaN

Below fineness 2.5 the Raymer term collapses (0.19 at λ = 2.18, an 81% under-count). Both the in-loop and post-loop paths now call the same helper; a gate fails any open-coded occurrence.

Capsule section — equivalent diameter

D_eq = √(W · H) π/4·D_eq² = π/4·W·H exactly area terms take D_eq; cabin floor and rotor clearance keep the true width

Reference cabin 1.5 m wide × 1.9 m tall: a circle of D = W is 21% low on frontal area, D = H is 27% high.

Cabin floor — occupancy constraint

fusLen · fusDiam ≥ occupants · 1.65 m² occupants = payload / 90.718 kg

90.718 kg = 200 lb: NASA/20180006683, "6 occupants at 1200 lb total." 1.65 m²: one CAD-measured cabin; population of eight (Yang 2024 Table 4, image estimates) spans 1.60–3.12 m²/occupant, mean 2.21 — the floor sits at the bottom of a real population.

Limit load factors — one home, every layout

manoeuvre n+ ≥ +2.0 , n− ≤ −0.5 MOC VTOL.2200(f) — certified FLOORS proposal default 3.5 / −1.5 sidebar (Structure), range +2..6 / −3..−0.5 — NO SOURCE winged n_limit = max(n+, gust cases) gusts 9.14 / 15.24 / 20.12 m/s (Enhanced) — MOC VTOL.2215(f) Δn = K_g·U_de·V·a·ρ0 / (2·W/S), K_g = 0.88μ/(5.3+μ) wingless n_limit = max(2.0, installed T/W) thrust-borne, the positive input is unused n_ult = 1.5 · n_limit VTOL.2230(a)(2) / AC 21.17-4 PL.2230(b) n_ult → AFDD wing and fuselage weights, inside the sizing loop

All of it lives in engine/loadcases.js; the certification rows, weight model, wing box, V-n and Performance tabs, exported report and regulation tracker read it from there, and the npm run loadfactor gate fails a copy anywhere else. EASA MOC SC-VTOL Issue 2, verbatim: "The positive load factor is not less than 2.0 and the negative limit manoeuvring load factor is not less than −0.5." The gust velocities are MOC VTOL.2215(f); the "currently not defined" sentence in MOC VTOL.2135 is the handling-qualities MOC, a different question. FAA AC 21.17-4 gives no load factor at all — its only requirement, PL.2200(b), is performance-based. The negative factor draws the V-n envelope and sizes no structure.

5 · The checks stage — 43 checks, 29 to 43 applicable per layout

The winged layouts run up to 43; the wingless ones 29 or 30, with the wing-borne criteria reported as not applicable rather than failed.

Convergence & feasibility

  • Sizing loop converged
  • Sizing loop stayed inside the design space
  • Weight-closure margin > 10%
  • Cruise range > 0 km
  • Usable pack ≥ mission energy
  • Battery fraction < 55%
  • Residual SoC at mission end
  • Cabin holds its occupants
  • Mission matches the reference aircraft

Certification

  • MTOW within the SC-VTOL-02 limit (5 700 kg)
  • Climb gradient ≥ 2.5% (MOC VTOL.2120, by category)

Rotor & hover

  • Blade loading C_T/σ < 0.12
  • Rotor solidity within buildable bound
  • Disk loading ≤ 25 lb/ft²
  • Tip Mach < 0.70
  • Hover download
  • Hover T/W ≥ 1.30
  • Hover load factor n_z ≥ 1.35 (continuous torque)
  • Hover thrust split within T/W
  • Hover trim: CG inside rotor array
  • Rotors even and ≥ 4 · rotors fit within the span

Failure, power & thermal

  • Controllable after a single rotor failure (ACAI)
  • Cruise thrust loss per failure < 40%
  • Motor peak capability ≥ OEI transient
  • Cruise duty < 80% continuous · cruise thermally balanced
  • Cell peak < 55 °C

Cruise & envelope

  • Cruise L/D ≥ 9.5 (advisory) · AR vs L/D compatible
  • Mach < 0.45
  • Descent speed inside the flight envelope
  • Static margin −10–25% MAC (advisory)
  • V-tail pitch and yaw authority

Structure & geometry

  • Structural margin of safety ≥ 0
  • Wing tip deflection < 15% semi-span
  • Boom mass < 15% of MTOW
  • Main tyre rated load · strut clears reserve-energy drop
  • Fus/Span 0.40–0.75 · Tail/Wing area 25–50%
  • Fuselage pitch moment in NP

6 · What is sourced, what is calibrated, what is not yet

Read this before trusting a number. Of 427 engine outputs, 14 (3%) have been compared numerically to a published aircraft. 131 are traced to a citation; 207 are derived; 6 are calibrated; 69 are unverified; none is unregistered. The weight buildup carries 5 [SRC], 12 [CAL], 6 [FRAC], 5 [LAY] tags; three of the twelve calibrations say in their own words that they have no published source.

Benchmark: 14.5% mean error over 39 NASA Table 12 comparisons, 13.8% over 11 commercial metrics (component buildup). AFDD's own equations carry 5–10% on the helicopters they were fitted to; this is conceptual-design fidelity, and a tool at this level claiming better than ~10% would be the suspicious one.

The programme to close the gaps, in order, is docs/VALIDATION-PROGRAM.md. The sixteen NASA weight statements and NASA's own calibration set are now in src/data/aircraft-database.js, tiered by pedigree.
